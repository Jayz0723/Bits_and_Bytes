Processing

Processor (CPU)

The CPU is the computer's main processor. It handles instructions for school work, programming, office applications, gaming frame rates, and video-editing timelines.

Best starting point
Modern 6-core processor
Important match
Motherboard socket
Buying tipMatch the CPU socket with the motherboard socket before ordering.
02

Graphics

Graphics Card (GPU)

The GPU renders games, 3D applications, video effects, and other visually demanding work. Office systems can often use integrated graphics instead of a separate card.

Most important for
Gaming and 3D work
Important match
Power supply and case
Buying tipMore powerful graphics cards require enough PSU wattage and physical case clearance.
03

Memory

Random Access Memory (RAM)

RAM keeps active applications and files ready for the processor. More memory improves multitasking and gives games and creative software additional working room.

Recommended
16GB to 32GB
Important match
DDR generation and speed
Buying tipDDR5 memory requires a DDR5-compatible motherboard and processor platform.
04

Storage

Solid State Drive (SSD)

The SSD stores Windows, applications, games, and personal files. A fast NVMe SSD reduces startup times and makes the computer feel more responsive.

Recommended
1TB NVMe SSD
Important match
M.2 size and interface
Buying tipOne terabyte is a practical starting capacity for most students, gamers, and office users.

Know your next upgrade

CPU — central processing unit
The processor handles instructions. Match its socket to your motherboard before buying.
RAM (ram) — random-access memory
Working memory for open apps. DDR4 and DDR5 are not interchangeable, and laptop SO-DIMMs differ from desktop DIMMs.
SSD — solid-state drive
Storage for files and games. Check the SATA or NVMe connection, physical size, and the slot your device supports.

Choose for your day

Study and office
Start with 16GB RAM and a 512GB SSD for everyday multitasking; check battery life for portable work.
Gaming and creative projects
Compare graphics performance, display resolution, and project storage. A larger number alone does not guarantee a better fit.

Before and after checkout

  1. Check your current device model and upgrade slots.
  2. Compare compatible parts and the exact capacity.
  3. Review quantities, delivery details, and the warranty.
  • Back up your files before changing storage.
  • Install drivers from the manufacturer's website.
  • Keep vents clear and save your purchase records.
